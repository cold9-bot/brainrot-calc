Formula assumptions

1 · Customer traffic / dayBase customers × (1 − price sensitivity × price change) × (1 + ad response × ad change) × (previous rating ÷ 4)
Price and ad changes are relative to their reference values. Defaults: sensitivity 1.5 and ad response 0.4. A 10% price increase at 1.5 sensitivity reduces the price factor by 15%.
2 · Peak rush / hour(customers / day × peak share) ÷ peak-window hours
Default: 55% of daily traffic across 2.5 hours.
3 · Staff stress / loadmin(100%, (peak customers / hour ÷ staff count) × load multiplier + low-wage morale points)
Morale points rise linearly below the reference wage (₹600 and up to 20 points by default; both are editable). Above the selected threshold (70% by default) is marked as burnout risk. With zero staff, load is 100%.
4 · Service waitbase wait (3 min) + stress percentage × 0.15
5 · Units, stockout, and ratingDemand = customers × avg items/order; whole units sold = floor(min(demand, inventory))
Stockout = demand > inventory; rating = clamp(1, 5, 5 − wait × 0.15 − (0.8 if stockout))
6 · Spoilage / dead stockmax(0, inventory − units sold) × wholesale cost × spoilage rate
Default spoilage: 30% cafe, 5% retail.
7 · Sales, expenses, and netSales = units sold × price (UPI + cash + card)
Costs = wholesale + staff wages + ads + spoilage + rent/utilities
Net profit/loss = sales − costs
8 · Next week and projectionsThis rating replaces the previous review score for next-week traffic.
Weekly = daily × 7; monthly = daily × 30 (same daily assumptions repeated).

All multipliers and business figures are editable assumptions, not measured Bengaluru market data. The example description contains conflicting traffic/rating outputs; the calculations use the formulas shown above.

This daily model repeats the same day for 7-day and 30-day totals. It does not model weekends, taxes, delivery-app commissions, payment mix, or product-specific prices and stock. The product table allocates total sold units by your estimated sales mix. Use it to explore relationships, not as a business forecast.
